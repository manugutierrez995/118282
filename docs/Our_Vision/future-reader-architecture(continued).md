# future-reader-architecture (continued)

> "The archive stores the work.
>
> The layers store understanding."

The true promise of the reader is not simply that it can display images.

Many programs can display images.

Many programs can draw rectangles.

The remarkable possibility is that the archive itself may become a foundation upon which countless independent layers of knowledge are built.

Imagine an editor.

Imagine a translator.

Imagine a historian.

Imagine an employee responsible for quality assurance.

Imagine a researcher studying visual symbolism.

None of them need alter the work.

Each contributes only a new layer.

The original page remains untouched.

Yet every contribution enriches the archive.

---

## Knowledge as Layers

Every annotation becomes more than a shape.

A polygon may describe the precise outline of a panel.

A shaded region may identify an important object.

A highlighted speech bubble may contain:

- corrected OCR
- multiple translations
- editorial commentary
- historical references
- character identities
- publication notes
- summaries
- links to related scenes

Different contributors may describe the same region from entirely different perspectives.

The archive gains depth without sacrificing integrity.

---

## Permissioned Knowledge

Not every layer belongs to everyone.

One layer may belong to translators.

Another to editors.

Another to moderators.

Another to researchers.

Another to trusted community contributors.

Permissions determine who may create, edit, review, approve, or publish each layer.

Knowledge becomes collaborative without becoming chaotic.

---

## Provenance

Every annotation should remember its history.

Who created it?

Who approved it?

When was it changed?

Which layer does it belong to?

Trust is preserved because provenance is preserved.

---

## Training Specialized Models

Perhaps the greatest opportunity lies beyond reading.

A carefully curated translation layer becomes training data for translation models.

Polygon panel outlines become training data for segmentation models.

OCR corrections become training data for recognition models.

Character identities become training data for visual recognition.

Editorial summaries become training data for summarization.

The archive ceases to be only a library.

It becomes a continuously growing source of structured human knowledge.

---

## The Compounding Effect

Knowledge compounds.

Every employee who contributes leaves behind more than completed work.

They leave behind structure.

Every carefully drawn polygon.

Every corrected sentence.

Every identified character.

Every linked scene.

Every summary.

Every relationship.

Each becomes another brick laid into a foundation that future humans—and future machines—may build upon.

The value of the archive therefore grows in two dimensions.

Its collection grows.

Its understanding grows.

The second may ultimately become the greater achievement.

---

## Closing Thought

An immutable archive is only the beginning.

The true achievement is creating a place where understanding may accumulate indefinitely without ever disturbing the original work.

Images preserve culture.

Layers preserve humanity's understanding of that culture.

The future reader is therefore not merely a reader.

It is an engine for the continual accumulation of knowledge.
